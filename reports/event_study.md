# Event study results — UK energy-policy announcements

**Status: pipeline complete, no estimates yet.** Two inputs are outstanding and
both are deliberately blocking rather than defaulted:

1. `data/events/uk_energy_policy_events.csv` ships header-only. Events are
   hand-curated (see [the curation guide](../data/events/README.md)).
2. `config/universe.yaml` does not load. Nine treated names from
   `docs/data_inventory.md` §7 carry `ticker: null`; the loader raises rather
   than guessing a symbol for a firm that merged or delisted mid-sample.

Everything below is either a pre-registered decision or a design-level result
that does not depend on the data. **The power section is a real finding
available today, and it is the most important thing in this document**: it
bounds what any result in the empty sections can possibly mean.

The results sections are generated by
[`reporting/event_study.py`](../src/policy_event_study/reporting/event_study.py)
and are not hand-written.

## 1. Reading guide: two rules this report is built around

**No point estimate appears without its placebo distribution.** This is
enforced in code, not by convention:
`EventResult.__post_init__` raises `MissingPlaceboError` if any estimator
contributed a τ without a matching permutation distribution. There is no flag
to disable it. A τ that has not been permuted cannot physically reach this
document.

**Events with high anticipation risk, a same-day confounder, or a recorded
leak are reported separately and never pooled.** Also structural: the pooled
section is built from `clean` and cannot see `flagged`. §6 explains what
anticipation does to identification rather than noting it and moving on.

## 2. Pre-registered design

Fixed before any estimation. Changing any of these after seeing results is a
new specification and gets a row in [`decision_log.md`](decision_log.md).

| Decision | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Estimation window | 250 trading days | `config/universe.yaml` `meta.min_pre_window_days` |
| Embargo (gap) | 30 trading days | Excludes the period over which UK policy is typically trailed in the press. Also the design's only out-of-sample pre-event holdout — see §5 |
| Post horizon | 20 trading days | Matches the longest window in the market-model ladder |
| Event windows (market model) | (−1,+1), (0,+1), (0,+5), (0,+20) | `docs/research_plan.md` Phase B1. Four windows is four looks; the count is in the decision log |
| Adjustment | `POINT_IN_TIME` | Raw closes + dated actions, applied forward only. See §7.1 |
| Clock alignment | `lag_late_markets` | `notes.timezone`. See §7.2 |
| SC/SDiD outcome | Cumulative sum of simple returns | `notes.matching_variable` bans price levels. Summation keeps the gap directly comparable with the market-model CAR |
| SDiD estimand | `HORIZON` | A single post period at t0+20, so τ is a CAR at the horizon and comparable with the other two |
| Significance level | 5%, two-sided unless `expected_direction` was curated in advance | §3 explains why 1% is not on the table |

### Event-day resolution

An announcement is located against the trading calendar using **both the open
and the close**, not the close alone:

- lands **before the open** (a 07:30 London press release) → that day's
  close-to-close return is entirely post-announcement, because no trading
  occurred inside the interval before it. `t0` is that day.
- lands **mid-session** → that day's return straddles the announcement. `t0`
  is the *next* day and the straddling day is reported separately.
- lands **after the close** → `t0` is the next trading day.
- **time not recovered** → the whole day is treated as straddling and the
  conservative convention is forced. `docs/data_inventory.md` §8 is explicit
  that GOV.UK gives date reliably and time less so; the honest response is to
  widen, not to guess.

## 3. Power: what this design could have detected

The section that decides how to read everything else.

### 3.1 The arithmetic floor — knowable with no data at all

A permutation test over `J` placebos cannot produce a p-value below
`1/(J+1)`. That is arithmetic, not an estimate.

| Donors `J` | Smallest attainable p |
|---|---|
| 23 (full pool) | **0.042** |
| 21 (events tagged `eu_efficiency_mandate`, which drop Nexity and Vonovia) | **0.045** |
| 19 | 0.050 |
| 15 | 0.063 |

| Threshold | Donors required |
|---|---|
| 10% | 9 |
| 5% | **19** |
| 1% | **99** |

**A 1% result is impossible in this design.** Not unlikely — impossible, for
any effect size, however large. Reaching it would need 99 plausibly untreated
donors, and the interference screen in `config/universe.yaml` does not admit
99 names. This report therefore states results at 5%, where the floor of 0.042
leaves exactly one rejection rung, and does not present 1% anywhere.

### 3.2 The noise floor — minimum detectable effect

The MDE is the smallest true effect that would be rejected at 5% with 80%
power, taking the in-space placebo distribution as the null and assuming a
true effect shifts it in location. The figures below come from the project's
own estimators run on simulated panels calibrated to plausible UK equity
volatility, and are **placeholders to be replaced with empirical placebo
spreads once prices are pulled** — the scale-free multiples at the bottom are
the part that will not change.

Post-SC daily gap volatility → detectable cumulative abnormal return, 23 donors:

| Daily gap σ | Horizon | SD(placebo τ) | 5% critical value | MDE @ 80% power |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.8% | 5d | 2.5% | 4.1% | **6.5%** |
| 0.8% | 20d | 4.3% | 7.9% | **11.8%** |
| 1.2% | 5d | 3.6% | 6.2% | **9.7%** |
| 1.2% | 20d | 6.4% | 11.8% | **17.7%** |
| 1.6% | 20d | 8.6% | 15.7% | **23.5%** |
| 2.0% | 20d | 10.7% | 19.6% | **29.4%** |

Scale-free, and this is the durable result:

> With 23 donors, the 5% critical value sits at **1.81 × SD(placebo τ)** and
> the MDE at 80% power at **2.68 × SD(placebo τ)**.

Pre-registering a direction buys real power: on the same panels the one-sided
MDE is **14.9%** against **17.7%** two-sided at the 20-day horizon. This is
why `expected_direction` is a curated column filled in before the returns are
seen, and why `ambiguous` is an honest answer with a cost attached.

**The plain reading.** At a 20-day horizon and realistic residual volatility,
this design detects a cumulative abnormal return somewhere around **12–24%**.
UK energy-efficiency announcements do not plausibly move a diversified
housebuilder by 20%. **The likely outcome is therefore a null that is
uninformative about effects of the size actually at stake**, and the correct
write-up says so rather than reporting "no significant effect" as though the
study had looked and found nothing.

Three levers, in order of how much they help:

1. **Shorten the horizon.** The MDE roughly halves from 20 days to 5 days,
   because the placebo spread grows with √horizon while a genuine
   announcement effect does not. A (0,+1) or (0,+5) window is far better
   powered, at the cost of missing slow repricing.
2. **Pool same-signed treated names** into one equal-weighted unit
   (`notes.power`, implemented as `Universe.signed_portfolio_members`).
   Raises signal-to-noise, but see §3.3 for how little it actually buys.
3. **Improve pre-treatment fit.** Every 1% reduction in daily gap volatility
   moves the MDE roughly proportionally. This is the strongest argument for
   spending effort on the donor pool rather than on estimators.

### 3.3 The correlation floor — effective observations

`CLAUDE.md` §3 requires effective independent observations, not row counts.
For `N` units with average pairwise correlation ρ, the design effect gives
`N_eff = N / (1 + (N−1)ρ)`:

| Treated names | ρ | Effective observations |
|---|---|---|
| 7 housebuilders | 0.7 | **1.35** |
| 7 housebuilders | 0.5 | 1.75 |
| 4 utilities | 0.7 | 1.29 |
| 8 mixed | 0.3 | 2.50 |

Seven UK housebuilders on a shared event date are worth about **1.4**
independent observations. A cross-sectional t-test treating them as seven
understates the standard error by roughly a factor of two — the concrete form
of the over-rejection `docs/research_plan.md` Phase B1 calls the single most
common error in published event studies. It is also why pooling (lever 2
above) helps much less than the name count suggests: pooling seven names into
one portfolio buys a √1.4 ≈ 1.2× improvement in signal-to-noise, not √7 ≈ 2.6×.

The market-model section reports three tests side by side for exactly this
reason: the naive cross-sectional t-test, the Brown–Warner crude dependence
adjustment, and the BMP standardised test. Where they disagree, the naive one
is wrong.

## 4. Estimators

Three, over one interface, so the comparison is like-for-like.

| | Counterfactual | Donor weights | Level matching |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Market model** | `α + β·r_market`, OLS on the estimation window | none | n/a |
| **Synthetic control** | Convex combination of donors | simplex, no intercept | required |
| **Synthetic DiD** | ω-weighted donors + λ-weighted pre-period offset | simplex + intercept + ridge ζ | relaxed to a DiD fixed effect |

The market model is the required `CLAUDE.md` §4 baseline and is implemented
first, with its own tests. It is not a straw man: it is included in every
event's run whether or not the caller asks for it, and it receives **the same
permutation inference as the other two**. Comparing an SC permutation p-value
against a market-model parametric t-test would confound the estimator with the
inference method; running both through the same permutation isolates the
estimator.

SDiD is in the study because Phase B2's kill criterion anticipates SC failing:
if UK housebuilders' exposure to UK rates and planning policy is not spanned
by any untreated combination, SC's simplex-with-no-intercept cannot fit, while
SDiD's intercept can still absorb a constant difference. **SC failing to span
is a reportable finding, not an error** — it is a substantive statement about
when synthetic control applies, and it answers the project's research question
directly.

## 5. Diagnostics attached to every estimate

| Diagnostic | Question | Failure means |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-treatment fit (RMSPE, normalised, vs market model) | Can the donor pool reconstruct this firm at all? | Phase B2 kill: the post-event gap is fit failure, not treatment |
| Pre-trend, in-sample slope (Newey–West) | Was the gap already drifting? | Good RMSPE with a slope is worse than mediocre RMSPE without one |
| Pre-trend, **embargo holdout** | Did it drift in the 30 days no estimator saw? | A leak or press trailing; the effective event date is earlier |
| In-space placebos | Would a different *firm* have shown this? | The primary p-value |
| In-time placebos | Would a different *date* have shown this? | The firm's gap wanders regardless of announcements |
| Leave-one-donor-out | Does one donor carry the whole result? | The estimate is a statement about that donor |
| Effective donor count (inverse Herfindahl) | How many donors does the counterfactual rest on? | Near 1 is fragile whatever LOO shows |

Two notes on how these are read.

**The embargo is a genuine holdout.** The 30 trading days between the
estimation window and `t0` are excluded from fitting but the gap *is* computed
over them, which makes them the only out-of-sample pre-event window the design
has. That is where a leak shows up first.

**The in-space exclusion ladder is reported in full.** Abadie's convention is
to drop placebo units whose pre-fit is far worse than the treated unit's, but
the cutoff is a researcher degree of freedom. Every event reports p at cutoffs
of none / 20× / 5× / 2×, so a result that only clears at one rung is visible
as such.

**The two placebo p-values are never combined.** They condition on different
things — unit given date, date given unit — and are not independent, so any
composite would understate the true p-value by an unknown amount. Both are
shown; where one passes and the other does not, that is the finding.

## 6. Anticipation and identification

`anticipation_risk: high` is not a quality flag. It is a statement that the
identification argument does not hold, and the report treats it that way.

**A high-anticipation event cannot produce an informative null.** If the news
was already in the price, there is no announcement-date effect to find, and a
zero estimate is equally consistent with "the policy did not move these firms"
and "it moved them a fortnight earlier, when it was trailed". The
announcement-date return cannot separate the two, and **no estimator in this
study can either** — the limitation lives in the event, not in the method.
Adding SC or SDiD on top does not recover a signal that was impounded before
the window opened.

What follows in practice:

- High-anticipation events are estimated and reported in full, with their
  placebo distributions, in the "reported separately" section.
- They are excluded from every pooled figure, structurally.
- Their estimates are read as a bound on the **residual surprise at the
  podium**, not as the policy's effect.
- The validator warns when `anticipation_risk` is `high` and both `leak_note`
  and `notes` are blank, because the report is required to discuss
  identification for these events and cannot do so from the flag alone.

The same separation applies to events with a same-day confounder. An
announcement made inside a Budget or a fiscal statement is essentially
uninterpretable for a firm-level event study: the donor pool cannot absorb a
shock that reaches UK domestic cyclicals through a different channel on the
same day. `docs/research_plan.md` Phase B0 warns that this is the *normal*
case for UK energy policy, and its kill criterion — fewer than ~8–10 clean
events means the panel design is not viable — exists because of it. The
documented fallback is a single-event deep dive, and taking it is not a
failure.

Given §3, there is a real chance that the clean-event count and the power
analysis both point the same way. If they do, the deliverable is the
methodological comparison plus the diagnostic finding, which is what
`docs/research_plan.md` says the project is for.

## 7. Known limitations

### 7.1 Corporate-action adjustment — smaller than it looks, and worth stating precisely

`docs/data_inventory.md` §7 warns that yfinance's `auto_adjust` rewrites
history retroactively, so a 2025 split alters 2020 prices. That is a real
`CLAUDE.md` §2.4 violation for price *levels*. For a returns-based study it
largely cancels:

Writing a back-adjusted price as `A_t = C_t / B_t` with `B_t = Π_{s>t} f_s`,

```
A_t / A_{t-1}  =  (C_t / C_{t-1}) · (B_{t-1} / B_t)  =  (C_t / C_{t-1}) · f_t
```

because `B_{t-1} = f_t · B_t`. Every factor dated after `t` cancels between
numerator and denominator, leaving only the factor effective on the day
itself — which is exactly what the point-in-time construction uses. This is
asserted as a test, not assumed.

Two consequences. First, `notes.matching_variable`'s ban on price levels is
load-bearing rather than stylistic: levels are where the contamination lives,
and the returns-based outcome is immune to it. Second, **the residual exposure
is factor *revision*, not factor *timing*** — Yahoo silently correcting a
split ratio or dividend after the fact does change past returns. The defence
is vintaged caching (the download date is in every cache filename); comparing
two vintages is the only way to measure it, and that comparison has not been
run.

### 7.2 Cross-market clock misalignment

Computed rather than assumed, from each exchange's close in its own zone under
both GMT and BST:

| Market | Offset vs LSE close | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| US (DHI, LEN, PHM, NVR, OC, CX) | **+4h30m** | Lagged one trading day, so each observation completed before the London close |
| Euronext Paris / Xetra | 0m | None needed — 17:30 CET *is* 16:30 London |
| Dublin | 0m | None needed |
| Zurich | −10m | Within tolerance |
| Copenhagen | −30m | **Uncorrectable** — see below |

The continental European closes are simultaneous with London's, which is worth
knowing: the naive assumption that "European donors need lagging too" would
have introduced a spurious one-day lag into 5 of 23 donors.

Copenhagen closes 30 minutes *before* London. Correcting that would require a
*lead* — future information — so it is disclosed rather than fixed. For an
announcement landing between 16:00 and 16:30 UTC, Rockwool's same-day close
misses news that London's captures.

### 7.3 Survivorship

yfinance does not serve delisted tickers, so the donor pool is built from firms
that exist today. This flatters pre-treatment fit and is carried as a hard flag
through every panel's provenance into every table. It is stated, not worked
around. Several UK mid-caps were acquired or delisted during the sample and are
absent; which ones is an open item for
[`docs/donor_pool_justification.md`](../docs/donor_pool_justification.md).

**Barratt Redrow needs specific care.** Barratt Developments and Redrow merged
in 2024, so the pre-merger sample is two separate series and the post-merger
series is a different firm. That is a corporate action the panel must handle
explicitly, not a ticker rename, and it is one reason the loader refuses to
guess symbols.

### 7.4 Data licence

Prices come from yfinance, an unofficial Yahoo Finance scraper with no
redistribution licence. Personal research use only; price data is gitignored
and never committed. If institutional access (WRDS, Datastream, Compustat) is
available through the university, `docs/data_inventory.md` §7 is right that
switching is the single biggest methodological upgrade available for zero
modelling work — it removes the licence problem and the survivorship problem
in one step.

### 7.5 Synthetic control non-uniqueness

With near-collinear sector peers the SC problem has a continuum of optimal
weight vectors, all with identical pre-treatment fit and different post-event
implications. A small ridge (`penalty=1e-6`, variance-scaled) selects the
minimum-norm solution so results are reproducible. Leave-one-donor-out is the
check on whether the choice among near-ties matters, and note that
concentration and instability are different failures: a pool of near-collinear
donors can be unconcentrated and still unstable.

## 10. What did not work

_Required by `CLAUDE.md` §5 and not optional. Populated as specifications are
tried; every row also appears in [`decision_log.md`](decision_log.md)._

Two entries already belong here, both from building the pipeline rather than
from estimation:

**A reconstructed `auto_adjust` mode that tested nothing.** The first
implementation rebuilt Yahoo's back-adjusted series from raw closes and the
action schedule. The algebra in §7.1 shows why that was circular: a pure
back-adjustment reduces to the point-in-time construction in returns, so the
"comparison" compared a series with itself. Replaced with Yahoo's own
`auto_adjust=True` series, fetched and stored alongside the raw close.

**A minimum-detectable-effect calculation that inverted the answer.** The
first version took both the critical value and the power quantile from the
absolute placebo distribution, which made a two-sided test appear *more*
powerful than a one-sided test on the same data. The critical value comes from
the statistic the test thresholds; the power quantile must come from the
signed distribution, because the alternative shifts ε and not |ε|. Every MDE
in §3.2 is post-fix, and a regression test now asserts the ordering.
